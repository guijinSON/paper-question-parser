# Research trajectory style contract

## Artifact class

`trajectory.md` is a simulated ideation transcript, not a report summary and not a theorem exposition.

It must be independently readable prose, but it remains downstream of the frozen factual bundle.

## Required style rules

- write in present tense
- open with live reading of the target paper rather than with a summary of the later answer
- use first person singular notebook voice
- make the opening concrete by reacting to actual pressure points or quoted passages already present in the factual bundle
- include many explicit attempted routes, not one smooth retrospective line
- include at least two hypothesis revisions
- include at least one moment where a search direction is abandoned because it is too weak, too broad, or unsupported by the bundle
- keep the prose independently readable without requiring the reader to cross-check `report.md`
- keep the body inside the mathematician's live ideation rather than stepping out to explain the contract
- end by synthesizing a mathematically plausible direction or method pressure rather than pretending to prove a final result

## Required narrative beats

1. Start by reading the actual target paper and noticing a concrete pressure point through bundle-backed evidence.
2. Move through multiple hypotheses, false starts, local confusions, and self-corrections.
3. Show neighboring search directions, but keep them bounded by topics and papers already present in the frozen bundle.
4. Let the emerging direction change shape during the transcript rather than appearing fully formed at the beginning.
5. End with a method-facing synthesis of how one might advance the mathematics from the observed pressures.

## Tone boundary

- no publicity prose
- no bland retrospective summary
- no claim to direct access to the authors' private thoughts
- no body-level contract narration such as `the bundle tells me`, `the contract does not allow`, or repeated self-policing about evidence discipline
- no polished success-only story that erases failures
- no descendant-aware triumphalism such as `this is exactly the later method` or `I have rediscovered the known solution`
- no intention-language unless directly supported, such as `the paper wants`, `the authors want`, `the paper tries`, or `the paper aims`

## Preferred phrasing for bounded bridges

- Prefer `I can read this as ...`, `this suggests ...`, or `this makes me try ...` over stronger teleological claims.
- Prefer `Plausible reconstruction:` or `Tentative route:` when moving from evidence to exploration.
- Prefer `this route collapses because ...` or `I cannot justify this from the paper yet` when abandoning a line.
- Prefer grounding the sentence in a local quoted pressure point, seed-ranking item, or ledger-backed claim rather than in broad historical claims about the field.

# Reconstructed monologue style contract

## Artifact class

`monologue.md` is a reconstructed discovery transcript, not a report summary.

It must be independently readable prose, but it remains downstream of the factual bundle.

## Required style rules

- write in present tense
- open with live reading of the top paper rather than with a summary of the final answer
- use first person singular notebook voice
- make the top-paper opening concrete by reacting to actual quoted passages from the factual bundle
- include actual quoted passages from the top paper and any directly read supporting paper that the factual bundle marked quote-eligible
- include 3-4 explicit attempted routes, not one smooth retrospective line
- include at least one hypothesis revision
- include at least one absurd moment that changes the question
- keep the prose independently readable without requiring the reader to cross-check `report.md`
- target 10k-20k tokens for a full compliant monologue
- make the conclusion derive the target paper's research topic or driving question from ancestor tensions rather than merely restating the finished theorem
- keep the body inside the mathematician's live reasoning rather than stepping out to explain contract discipline

## Required narrative beats

1. Start by reading the actual top paper and noticing a concrete pressure point through an actual quoted passage.
2. Move through multiple hypotheses, false starts, local confusions, and self-corrections.
3. Show route changes in real time rather than flattening them into hindsight.
4. Let the eventual reconstructed research topic or question emerge through the search, not as the first sentence.
5. End by deriving why this target paper had to ask *this* research question, given the bounded ancestor pressures in the bundle.

## Quote rule

When the monologue uses actual quoted passages, they must be drawn from quote-eligible claims in the factual bundle. Do not substitute vague paraphrases when the contract requires a quote-bearing moment.

## Tone boundary

- no publicity prose
- no bland retrospective summary
- no vague instruction like "be more reflective"
- no claim to direct access to the authors' private thoughts
- no body-level contract narration such as "the bundle tells me", "the contract does not allow", "I need to check myself against the bundle", or repeated self-policing about evidence discipline
- no repeated disclaimer recitals inside the body
- no intention-language unless directly supported, such as `the target wants`, `the paper wants`, `the authors want`, `the paper tries`, or `the paper aims`
- no overconfident synthesis lines such as `this is where all the strands meet`, `the real point is`, or `everything converges here` unless the relation is explicitly bundle-backed

## Preferred phrasing for bounded bridges

- Prefer `I can read this as ...`, `this reads to me as ...`, or `this looks like ...` over intention-language such as `the paper wants ...`
- Prefer `this move becomes legible here` or `these strands seem to come into closest alignment here` over teleological claims such as `this is where all the strands meet`
- Prefer `Plausible reconstruction:` when moving from explicit evidence to an interpretive bridge
- Prefer `appears`, `suggests`, `supports reading`, `frames`, or `presents` over stronger verbs like `proves`, `reveals`, `shows that the real point is`, or `must have intended`
- Prefer grounding the sentence in a local object such as `this passage`, `this quoted pressure point`, `this move`, or `this comparison` rather than in broad historical objects such as `the paper`, `the field`, or `the research topic`

Any speculative bridge must remain visibly speculative, but the required disclaimer should appear once in a fixed opening note rather than recurring inside the monologue body.
